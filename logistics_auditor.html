The "Last Mile" Logistics Auditor¶

This notebook checks Veridi Logistics' delivery data to find out why customer reviews are getting worse. We go step by step: explore the data, join the tables, measure delays, compare states, and link delays to reviews.

Step 0: Setup¶

We import the libraries we need. The data files must be in a data/olist folder next to this notebook.

4.3 Label each order¶

On Time = arrived on or before the promised date, Late = 1 to 5 days late, Super Late = more than 5 days late. Orders that never arrived get the label "Not Delivered".

In [18]:
def get_status(days):
    if pd.isnull(days):
        return "Not Delivered"
    elif days >= 0:
        return "On Time"
    elif days >= -5:
        return "Late"
    else:
        return "Super Late"

master["Delivery_Status"] = master["Days_Difference"].apply(get_status)
master["is_late"] = master["Delivery_Status"].isin(["Late", "Super Late"])

Conclusion:

  • Only 6.8% of delivered orders are late, but late orders get much worse reviews: 4.29 for on time, 2.99 for late, and 1.74 for super late. About 76% of super late orders get a 1 or 2 star review, so late delivery clearly hurts customer happiness.
  • The problem is not nationwide. It is centered in the Northeast (12.7% late, about double the South), with Alagoas (21.4%), Maranhão (17.4%) and Sergipe (15.2%) the worst states.
  • Remote states are hit harder overall: every Northeast state and the North as a whole (8.6%, mostly Pará at 11.2%) are above the national average. But distance is not the whole story, because far-off Amazonas, Rondônia, Amapá and Acre are among the best states.
  • On average we promise 23 days but deliver in 12, so most estimates are too long, not too short. The real issue is a few regions and periods: in March 2018, 19% of orders were late, and review scores dropped in the same months.
